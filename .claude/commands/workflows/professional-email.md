## Streamlined Email Workflow

This workflow helps compose professional emails quickly while maintaining quality, appropriate tone, and clear communication.

### Phase 1: Context Analysis (analyze-agent)

**Use analyze-agent** to understand the situation:

1. **Email Thread Context** (if replying):
   - What's the conversation history?
   - What questions need answering?
   - What commitments were made?
   - What's the current tone/mood?

2. **Relationship Context**:
   - Who is the recipient? (role, seniority, relationship history)
   - Internal or external?
   - First contact or ongoing relationship?
   - Professional distance level? (close colleague vs formal executive)

3. **Urgency Assessment**:
   - Time-sensitive? (needs response by when?)
   - Priority level? (critical, important, routine, FYI)
   - Are others waiting on this?

4. **Cultural/Organizational Context**:
   - Company culture? (formal, casual, somewhere in between)
   - Geographic/cultural considerations?
   - Industry norms? (legal, healthcare = formal; startups = casual)
   - Organizational hierarchy?

**Deliverable**: Context brief with recipient profile, urgency, cultural norms

**Quality Gate 1**: Context understood?
- [ ] Recipient relationship clear
- [ ] Urgency level determined
- [ ] Cultural norms identified
- [ ] Thread context reviewed (if applicable)

---

### Phase 2: Goal Identification

**Define what you want to achieve**:

1. **Primary Purpose**:
   - **Request Information**: Need data, answer, clarification
   - **Provide Information**: Share update, answer question, report status
   - **Request Action**: Need approval, task completion, meeting
   - **Build Relationship**: Networking, follow-up, thank you
   - **Resolve Issue**: Address problem, complaint, misunderstanding
   - **Coordinate**: Schedule, align, plan together

2. **Desired Outcome**:
   - What response do you want?
   - By when do you need it?
   - What happens next after this email?

3. **Success Criteria**:
   - How will you know this email succeeded?
   - What action should recipient take?
   - What understanding should they have?

**Deliverable**: Clear goal statement with success criteria

**Quality Gate 2**: Goal clear?
- [ ] Primary purpose identified
- [ ] Desired outcome specific
- [ ] Success criteria defined

---

### Phase 3: Tone Selection

**Choose appropriate tone for context**:

1. **Formality Spectrum**:
   - **Formal** (executives, legal, first contact, sensitive topics)
     - "Dear Dr. Smith," "I am writing to request..."
   - **Professional** (colleagues, business partners, standard business)
     - "Hi Sarah," "I wanted to follow up on..."
   - **Casual** (close colleagues, internal teams, creative industries)
     - "Hey team," "Quick question about..."

2. **Approach Spectrum**:
   - **Direct** (clear ask, urgent, transactional)
     - Get to point quickly, state what you need
   - **Diplomatic** (sensitive issue, upward management, difficult news)
     - Build up, provide context, soften asks
   - **Friendly** (relationship building, networking, appreciation)
     - Warm, personal, conversational

3. **Tone Markers**:
   - Formal: "I would appreciate...", "Please find attached...", "Thank you for your consideration"
   - Professional: "Could you...", "Attached is...", "Thanks"
   - Casual: "Can you...", "Here's...", "Thanks!"

4. **Tone Consistency Check**:
   - Does tone match relationship?
   - Does tone match content seriousness?
   - Is tone consistent throughout?
   - Does tone match company culture?

**Deliverable**: Tone profile (formality level + approach + key markers)

**Quality Gate 3**: Tone appropriate?
- [ ] Matches relationship level
- [ ] Matches content seriousness
- [ ] Matches organizational culture
- [ ] Consistent throughout email

---

### Phase 4: Email Composition

**Compose email following best practices**:

1. **Subject Line**:
   - Specific and clear (not vague)
   - Indicates urgency if needed: "[Action Required]", "[Time-Sensitive]"
   - Summarizes content: "Q3 Budget Approval Needed by Friday"
   - Actionable: "Please Review: Marketing Proposal"

2. **Opening/Greeting**:
   - Match formality: "Dear Dr./Hi/Hey [Name],"
   - Consider context: "Hope you're doing well" vs get straight to point
   - Reference previous interaction if applicable

3. **First Sentence (Purpose Statement)**:
   - State purpose immediately
   - Why you're writing
   - What you need
   - Example: "I'm writing to request your approval for the Q3 marketing budget."

4. **Body (1-3 short paragraphs)**:
   - **Context**: Background they need (briefly)
   - **Details**: Specific information, data, questions
   - **Action Items**: What you need them to do (numbered list if multiple)

   **Best Practices**:
   - Keep paragraphs short (2-4 sentences)
   - Use bullet points for multiple items
   - Bold key information
   - One main idea per paragraph

5. **Closing/Call-to-Action**:
   - Restate what you need
   - Include deadline if applicable
   - Make action clear and easy
   - Example: "Please approve by Friday, December 15. Simply reply 'Approved' to proceed."

6. **Sign-off**:
   - Match formality: "Best regards," (formal) / "Thanks," (professional) / "Cheers," (casual)
   - Include signature block with contact info

**Email Structure Template**:
```
Subject: [Clear, specific, actionable]

[Greeting],

[Purpose statement - why I'm writing]

[Context paragraph - background if needed]

[Details/Request paragraph with specifics]

[Action items]:
1. [What I need from you]
2. [By when]
3. [How to respond]

[Closing sentence - thank you, next steps]

[Sign-off],
[Your name]
[Title, contact info]
```

**Deliverable**: Complete draft email

**Quality Gate 4**: Email well-structured?
- [ ] Subject line clear
- [ ] Purpose stated upfront
- [ ] Body concise (under 200 words ideal)
- [ ] Action items clear
- [ ] Deadline specified (if applicable)

---

### Phase 5: Clarity & Brevity Check

**Ensure email is clear and concise**:

1. **Clarity Tests**:
   - Can recipient understand what you need without re-reading?
   - Are action items unambiguous?
   - Is technical jargon defined or avoided?
   - Are pronouns clear? (watch "it", "this", "they")

2. **Brevity Edits**:
   - Remove redundant phrases ("in order to" → "to")
   - Cut filler words ("actually", "basically", "just")
   - Eliminate unnecessary qualifiers ("very", "really", "quite")
   - Combine sentences where possible
   - Cut to-the-point: Subject line + 3 sentences is often enough

3. **Scannability**:
   - Can they skim and get the point?
   - Are key points highlighted (bold/bullets)?
   - Is most important info at top?
   - Is there white space (not a wall of text)?

4. **One-Main-Ask Rule**:
   - Ideally, one primary request per email
   - If multiple asks, prioritize and number them
   - Don't hide important requests in middle

**Deliverable**: Refined, concise email

**Quality Gate 5**: Clear and concise?
- [ ] Under 200 words (unless complex topic)
- [ ] One main point/ask
- [ ] Scannable structure
- [ ] No unnecessary words

---

### Phase 6: Proofread & Polish (proofread-agent)

**Use proofread-agent** for final check:

1. **Grammar & Spelling**:
   - No typos (especially in recipient name!)
   - Correct grammar
   - Proper punctuation
   - Capitalization consistency

2. **Tone Verification**:
   - Reads professionally
   - Not too casual or too formal
   - Not passive-aggressive
   - Positive/neutral (avoid negative language)

3. **Completeness**:
   - All questions answered (if replying)?
   - All necessary info included?
   - Attachments mentioned and attached?
   - Links work?

4. **Professional Polish**:
   - No ALL CAPS (looks like shouting)
   - No excessive exclamation points
   - No emoji (unless very casual context)
   - Consistent formatting

**Deliverable**: Proofread, polished email

**Quality Gate 6**: Ready to send?
- [ ] No errors
- [ ] Tone appropriate
- [ ] All info included
- [ ] Attachments attached
- [ ] Professional presentation

## Email Type Templates

### Request for Information

```
Subject: Quick Question About [Topic]

Hi [Name],

I'm [context - why you need this info]. Could you share [specific information] by [date]?

Specifically, I need:
1. [Item 1]
2. [Item 2]

This will help me [why it matters/what you'll do with it].

Thanks!
[Your name]
```

**Tone**: Professional, direct, appreciative

---

### Status Update

```
Subject: [Project Name] - Weekly Update

Hi team,

Quick update on [project]:

✅ Completed this week:
- [Item 1]
- [Item 2]

🚧 In progress:
- [Item 3]

⚠️ Blockers:
- [Issue] - need [help needed] by [date]

Next week: [What's coming]

Let me know if questions!

[Name]
```

**Tone**: Professional, clear, action-oriented

---

### Meeting Request

```
Subject: Meeting Request: [Topic] - [Proposed Date/Time]

Hi [Name],

I'd like to discuss [topic] with you. Could we meet for 30 minutes [proposed time]?

Agenda:
1. [Topic 1] (10 min)
2. [Topic 2] (15 min)
3. [Topic 3] (5 min)

I'll send calendar invite if this works. If not, please suggest alternative times.

Thanks,
[Name]
```

**Tone**: Professional, respectful, clear

---

### Difficult Conversation

```
Subject: Discussing [Issue]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to address [issue] that came up [when/where].

[Brief context - what happened]

I understand [acknowledge their perspective]. From my side, [your perspective].

I'd like to [proposed solution/next step]. Could we discuss this [when]?

I appreciate your time on this.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

**Tone**: Diplomatic, respectful, solution-focused

---

### Thank You / Appreciation

```
Subject: Thank You for [Specific Thing]

Hi [Name],

I wanted to thank you for [specific action/help]. It really helped me [specific outcome].

I especially appreciated [particular aspect]. It made a big difference to [why it mattered].

Looking forward to [next interaction/future collaboration].

Thanks again!
[Name]
```

**Tone**: Warm, specific, genuine

---

### Follow-Up

```
Subject: Re: [Original Subject] - Following Up

Hi [Name],

Following up on my email from [date] about [topic].

To recap: I need [what you need] by [when] for [reason].

Could you update me on status? If there are any blockers, happy to help resolve.

Thanks,
[Name]
```

**Tone**: Professional, persistent but polite

## Common Email Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Burying the Lede**:
   ❌ Don't: Write long context before stating purpose
   ✅ Do: State purpose in first sentence

2. **Vague Subject Lines**:
   ❌ Don't: "Question" / "Update" / "Thoughts?"
   ✅ Do: "Budget Approval Needed by Friday" / "Q3 Sales Report" / "Feedback on Proposal Draft"

3. **Novel-Length Emails**:
   ❌ Don't: Write 500-word emails
   ✅ Do: Keep under 200 words; if longer, use bullets/formatting

4. **Unclear Action Items**:
   ❌ Don't: "Let me know your thoughts" (what does this mean?)
   ✅ Do: "Please review and approve by Friday" (specific action)

5. **Passive Aggressive Tone**:
   ❌ Don't: "Per my last email..." / "As I mentioned before..."
   ✅ Do: "Following up on [topic]..." / "To clarify..."

6. **Reply-All Abuse**:
   ❌ Don't: Reply-all unless everyone needs to see it
   ✅ Do: Reply only to people who need the info

7. **No Deadline**:
   ❌ Don't: "Please let me know when you can"
   ✅ Do: "Please respond by Friday, Dec 15"

8. **Too Many Questions**:
   ❌ Don't: Ask 10 questions in one email
   ✅ Do: Ask 1-3 questions; use bullets; prioritize

## Tips for Different Scenarios

### Emailing Up (To Boss/Executive)
- Be brief (they're busy)
- Lead with bottom line
- Provide recommendation, not just problem
- Make it easy to say yes/no
- Include "No response needed" if FYI

### Emailing Down (To Direct Reports)
- Be clear about expectations
- Provide context for "why"
- Specify deadlines and priorities
- Offer support if needed
- Be encouraging and respectful

### Emailing Across (To Peers/Colleagues)
- Be collaborative, not demanding
- Acknowledge their priorities
- Offer to reciprocate help
- Keep it friendly but professional
- Use "we" language ("let's", "we can")

### Emailing Externally (Clients/Partners)
- Extra professional tone
- Assume they don't know internal context
- Spell out acronyms
- Triple-check for errors
- Include full signature block

### Difficult/Sensitive Topics
- Don't send when emotional (draft, wait, revise)
- Focus on facts, not feelings
- Assume good intent
- Propose solutions, not just problems
- Consider if phone call is better
