## 💡 Pro Tips for Research Use Cases

### Literature Review Tips
1. **Start broad, narrow down**: General topic → specific questions
2. **Track sources**: Save workflow output for citations
3. **Iterate**: Multiple research workflows for depth
4. **Verify claims**: Workflows find patterns, you verify with sources

### Grant Writing Tips
1. **Address all criteria**: NSF, NIH have specific requirements
2. **Show broader impact**: Connect research to societal benefit
3. **Be realistic**: Conservative timelines and budgets
4. **Get feedback**: Workflow draft, then peer review

### Academic Writing Tips
1. **Use for structure**: Workflow handles outline and flow
2. **Add your voice**: Customize with your insights and analysis
3. **Cite properly**: Workflow provides sources, you format citations
4. **Iterate**: Multiple drafts, workflow accelerates each one

## ❓ FAQ for Researchers

**Q: Does this violate academic integrity?**
A: No. Workflows do literature review and drafting—you provide original analysis, insights, and verification. Like having a research assistant.

**Q: Can I cite workflow-generated research?**
A: No. Cite the original sources the workflow found. Always verify sources yourself.

**Q: What about novel research?**
A: Workflows handle background/literature. Your novel contributions are still yours.

**Q: Will reviewers know I used AI?**
A: Not if you customize and add your analysis. Final work should reflect your expertise.

**Q: How do I handle citations?**
A: Workflow provides sources in markdown. Convert to your citation style (APA, Chicago, etc.).
