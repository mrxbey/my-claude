## Translation Approach

### Strategy
- **Target Audience**: Latin American business professionals and decision-makers
- **Formality Level**: Professional but approachable (business-to-business)
- **Register**: Use formal "usted" form throughout
- **Cultural Adaptation**: Prioritize Latin American Spanish conventions

### Key Decisions

#### 1. Product Tier Names

**"Business Plan" vs "Enterprise Plan"**

**Decision**:
- Business Plan → "Plan Empresarial" (translated)
- Enterprise Plan → "Plan Enterprise" (kept in English)

**Rationale**:
The tech industry has standardized on "Enterprise" as the premium tier name globally. Keeping it in English:
- Maintains brand consistency across markets
- Aligns with industry expectations (Enterprise = top tier)
- Resonates with target audience (technical decision-makers familiar with English terms)

**Alternative considered**: "Plan Corporativo" (fully translated)
- More linguistically correct
- But less recognizable to target audience
- Chose brand recognition over linguistic purity

---

#### 2. Technical Terms Translation

**"Seamlessly" → "sin problemas"**

**Context**: "work together seamlessly" / "trabajar juntos sin problemas"

**Decision**: Translated to Spanish idiom

**Alternatives considered**:
- "perfectamente" (perfectly) - too absolute
- "fluidamente" (fluidly) - too technical
- "sin inconvenientes" (without inconveniences) - too wordy
- "sin problemas" (without problems) - ✅ **CHOSEN**: Natural, conversational, business-appropriate

**Rationale**: While the most literal translation might be "perfectamente integrado", "sin problemas" captures the spirit better for Latin American audiences.

---

#### 3. Idiomatic Expressions

**"Game-changing" → "revolucionaria"**

**Context**: Customer testimonial about real-time editing

**Decision**: Cultural adaptation, not literal translation

**Alternatives considered**:
- "que cambia el juego" - awkward literal translation
- "transformadora" (transformative) - good but less impactful
- "revolucionaria" (revolutionary) - ✅ **CHOSEN**: Stronger impact, culturally appropriate

**Rationale**: Sports metaphors don't always translate well. "Game-changing" is very US-centric. "Revolucionaria" captures the disruptive innovation concept better for Spanish speakers.

---

#### 4. Privacy and Legal Terms

**"GDPR compliance" → "cumplimiento con RGPD"**

**Decision**: Translate acronym, use "cumplimiento con" not "cumplimiento de"

**Details**:
- GDPR = RGPD (Reglamento General de Protección de Datos)
- Preposition: "con" (with) emphasizes adherence
- Alternative: "de" (of) is also correct but slightly less natural

**Consistency**:
- "SOC 2 certification" → kept as "certificación SOC 2" (acronym widely recognized)
- "HIPAA" → kept as acronym (universally known in healthcare)

---

#### 5. Call-to-Action Phrasing

**"Start your free trial today" → "Comience su prueba gratuita hoy"**

**Decision**: Formal command form (usted)

**Alternatives**:
- "Comienza tu prueba..." (informal tú) - rejected, too casual for B2B
- "Inicia tu prueba..." (informal, different verb) - rejected
- "Comience su prueba..." (formal usted) - ✅ **CHOSEN**: Appropriate formality

**Consistency check**: All CTAs use formal "usted" form:
- "Regístrese" (Sign up)
- "Invite a su equipo" (Invite your team)
- "Cree su primer espacio" (Create your first workspace)
- "Explore las funciones" (Explore features)

## Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge 1: "Notification Fatigue"

**Issue**: Relatively new term in English, no established Spanish equivalent

**Solution**: Direct translation "fatiga de notificaciones"
- Transparent meaning
- Follows Spanish word formation patterns
- Alternative "sobrecarga de notificaciones" (notification overload) considered but less precise

### Challenge 2: "Bank-Level Encryption"

**Issue**: Trust-building metaphor might not translate culturally

**Solution**: Kept metaphor as "encriptación de nivel bancario"
- Banks are universally trusted in LatAm for security
- Metaphor works cross-culturally
- Maintains marketing impact

### Challenge 3: Pricing Currency

**Issue**: Source uses "$" - ambiguous in LatAm (many countries use $)

**Solution**: Kept $ symbol without USD specification
- International SaaS pricing commonly shown in USD
- Context makes it clear (US company, .com domain)
- Standard practice in LatAm tech market

## Regional Variations Considered

### Latin America vs Spain

**Vocabulary Choices**:
- "computadora" (LatAm) chosen over "ordenador" (Spain)
- "correo electrónico" (universal) chosen over "email" (anglicism)
- "encriptación" (LatAm) chosen over "cifrado" (Spain)

**Pronoun Forms**:
- "ustedes" (LatAm, universal) used throughout
- "vosotros" (Spain specific) deliberately avoided
- Verb conjugations match LatAm standard

**Rationale**: Primary target is Latin America (larger market, higher growth), but translation is fully comprehensible to Spanish readers.
